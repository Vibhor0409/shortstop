0The problem and who has it

CPG brands selling to Walmart, Amazon, Target, Kroger and Costco routinely get paid less than they invoiced. Retailers deduct for shortages, late trucks, pricing, promotions and compliance, each in its own portal with its own codes and dispute window. Fighting one means finding a signed proof of delivery (POD), matching it to the PO and invoice and retyping it, so small ones get written off (97% of brands auto-write-off small deductions, per the Credit Research Foundation’s 2023 survey).

Primary user: the AR or deduction analyst at a $10M–$100M brand juggling portal logins and a spreadsheet. Approver: the finance lead who signs off on large disputes. Later: the CFO, who wants one recovered-dollars number that matches the ERP.

2Product implementation

Sources (read-only)

  • Retailer portals: Amazon Vendor Central, Walmart Retail Link, Target Partners Online, Kroger, Costco. Credentials vaulted; RPA fallback where there is no API.
  • ERP: NetSuite, QuickBooks Online, Xero, Sage Intacct through a unified accounting API (Apideck).
  • Carrier, 3PL and email for PODs and remittance advice.
  • Ramp Developer API to match recoveries to deposits.
→

Shortstop core

  1. Normalise into one shipment-and-payment record, deduplicated by PO + invoice + retailer.
  2. Four-way match: PO vs ASN vs POD vs invoice. Guard runs it before pickup; Recover runs it after payment.
  3. Classify and rank each short-paid line by deadline, then amount × win probability.
  4. Package evidence: POD OCR (Mindee) plus field extraction, with a source location for every value.
  5. Draft from retailer-format templates as a structured proposal with an idempotency key, so nothing is filed twice.
→

Outputs

  • Guard alert before pickup
  • Ranked deduction queue
  • Filed dispute in the retailer’s format
  • Recovered-dollar ledger synced to the ERP and Ramp
  • Leakage by retailer, recovery rate, missed windows (target 0)
Human gate:Proposed→Pending review→Approved · Edited · Rejected→Filing→Filed→Paid or denied

How the AI is kept in check

The model drafts and cites; it has no permission to file. Every reason ends in a citation chip that opens the source line. With fewer than 3 of 3 proofs it says “not enough to dispute yet” and will not draft.

How confidence is stated

Never a bare score. “98% match on the POD signature · all 3 documents found” names its basis. Analysts correct it with Teach Shortstop, and each saved rule is listed in Settings and can be switched off.

Permissions and security

Read-only by default. Maker-checker above $1,500: the preparer can never approve. “File without a person” is locked off. Every step logs approver, time and document versions.

Build vs buy, and the MVP line

Buy plumbing (ERP connectors, OCR, money data). Build what wins disputes: match engine, ranking, retailer templates, approval gate. MVP: 5 retailers, 2 flows, a human on every filing.

4Future plans

The flywheel we are betting on

Every dispute Recover wins teaches Guard a rule

Amazon PO 4388 went out with a 480-vs-500 ASN miscount and came back as a $1,240 shortage claim. Recover wins it back, and Guard now checks every ASN for that mistake before pickup: Walmart PO 4471 is caught at 480 vs 500 and a $360 claim never exists. Over time the product moves spend from recovery to prevention, which is where most of the value sits.

Where it goes next
  • Inside Ramp: recovered deposits reconcile automatically, and “money in” sits beside Bill Pay’s “money out” in one cash picture.
  • Promo and trade spend: validate promotion deductions against the agreement before they are taken.
  • Benchmarks: anonymised win rates by retailer and deduction type, so a brand knows which fights are worth having.
  • Earned autonomy: auto-file only for a rule a team has approved many times, under a threshold they set, always with Undo and an audit trail.

What we would test with design partners first

  • Recovery rate: is 60–80% of disputable dollars real, per retailer? This is our least certain number.
  • Evidence coverage: how often are all 3 proofs findable without a person chasing a carrier?
  • Trust in the read: do analysts open the citation, and how often do they correct the AI or teach a rule?
  • Guard timing: does an alert at ASN creation leave enough time before pickup to fix the paperwork?
  • Dispute windows: they differ by retailer and change; the demo shows “Set by retailer · verify” until confirmed.

When things go wrong

  • Missing POD: the item stays in the queue as “not enough evidence” while Shortstop requests it from the carrier.
  • Portal disconnects: the source is flagged, affected deductions are marked possibly out of date, one click reconnects.
  • Analyst rejects: a reason code feeds the next ranking. Retailer denies: the outcome is logged and feeds win rates.
  • Filed by mistake: Undo is offered at the moment of filing, and the audit trail records both actions.

Benefit, quoted as a range

$72k – $336kper year for a $10M brand · $175k at the midpoint

$10M revenue × 3–7% deducted (Finortal, 2025) × 40–60% disputable (industry estimate) × 60–80% recovered (our assumption, to validate with design partners). Plus time (hours of assembly become a short review), risk (no missed windows) and one cash number that reconciles to the ERP and Ramp.

Success metrics

Dollars recovered per brand · recovery rate on disputable deductions · missed dispute windows = 0 · time to file · Guard catches per month (deductions that never happened). North star: net recovered dollars per analyst-hour.

Risks, named up front

Retailer portals have no public APIs, so connectors are brittle. OCR makes mistakes, which is why the human gate and source links exist. Recovery rates are assumptions until design partners prove them. Market proof: a published case study reports Miss Jones Baking Co. winning a single $22,943.83 Walmart shortage dispute and finding $120,000 in unclaimed invalid deductions.
